      Board Chair’s letter

      Forward Progress

      As a football fan, I am always heartened when credit is given for the forward progress of the runner or receiver, even though they may have been tackled and dragged backward for several yards before the play is blown dead. Institutions can sometimes get tackled and dragged backward too, but I am pleased to report that this year our gains have outweighed our losses! Although I am entering my last year as board chair, I feel more optimistic than ever about ICS. Here are some reasons why:

      • We made a very significant and seamless transition from Dr. Ron Kuipers to Dr. Beth Green as president of ICS. I am grateful to Ron for his sense that it was time to return to his love of scholarship and the classroom, and to hand over the reins to Beth. I am grateful to Beth for answering the call to work at a place where she feels fully mission-aligned. Kudos to both Ron and Beth for working so smoothly and humbly together for the benefit of the institution and for doing everything needed to help ICS thrive!
      • We are engaging with old friends and new in ever-increasing ways through our people and our programs. Old friends who wondered what had happened to us have responded with interest and financial support in appreciation of our new direction. New friends are coming to us through our Free to be Faithful courses, our Substack, and the visibility of our faculty, and they are finding out how much they enjoy our lively and welcoming community.
      • As a measure of our renewed engagement, our donations increased by 50% last year! This was in spite of being knocked off balance just as we had approved our budget; a large grant we had been counting on fell through, and we were left with a large funding gap to fill. Through the hard work of our advancement team, the fiscal responsibility of our staff, and the supportive response of our donors, old and new, we finished the fiscal year with the smallest deficit in recent memory!
      • We are reaching out to explore new ways of serving. Our outreach to pastors, particularly those in disaffiliating CRC churches, has been greatly appreciated. We will continue to form networks and create resources to support pastors and churches. We continue to enjoy collaborative relationships with a growing number of organizations with which we can find common cause.

      Thank you for your interest and support, and for cheering us on as an ICS community to continue to be faithful! Your encouragement helps us get up and line up again. We press on with an open heart to all, asking the hard questions without fear, pondering and wondering together what it means to follow the way of Christ.

      Sincerely,

      Dan Beerens
      Chair, Board of Trustees
      Institute for Christian Studies

      Draft minutes of the 2025 AGM

      The annual general meeting of the Institute for Christian Studies was held on Zoom on Saturday, December 6, 2025. These draft minutes come up for approval at this year’s meeting, under item 6 of the agenda.

      Read the minutesHide the minutes 11 items

      1. 1 Welcome and opening prayer

        Dan Beerens chaired the meeting and welcomed participants. Ron Kuipers offered the opening prayer, giving thanks for the Institute and its supporters and asking for wisdom, justice, and faithful imagination in its work.

      2. 2 Statement of reconciliation and land acknowledgment

        Ron Kuipers offered a personal statement of reconciliation, reflecting on his upbringing in Treaty 6 territory, the responsibilities of settler Canadians, and the need to receive Indigenous histories as part of their own story. He described ICS’s efforts to incorporate more BIPOC voices in the curriculum and concluded with a land acknowledgment and a commitment to learning, repentance, and action.

      3. 3 Chair’s remarks

        Dan Beerens reflected on developments since the previous AGM and affirmed the Institute’s commitment to be bold and thrive. He highlighted the increased engagement arising from ICS’s decision to end its denominational relationship with the Christian Reformed Church in North America and from the Free to be Faithful program. He emphasized the importance of a welcoming academic community in which members can ask difficult questions and disagree while deepening faith.

      4. 4 Adoption of the agenda

        The Chair proposed moving the remarks from fundraising consultant Markku Kostamo immediately after the President’s report, ahead of the finance report.

      5. 5 Reception of the 2024 AGM minutes

        Two corrections were presented: the meeting date was corrected to December 7, 2024, and the description of ICS’s three pillars was corrected to identify the theory-focused MA and PhD in philosophy, the educational leadership stream of the MA program, and lifelong learning opportunities under the Master of Worldview Studies program.

      6. 6 President’s report

        Ron Kuipers presented the President’s report on the 2024–25 academic and fiscal year and subsequent developments. He described ICS’s mission as cultivating a spacious and hopeful Christian imagination in a polarized world.

        The report covered the Institute’s February 2025 decision to end its formal denominational relationship with the CRCNA while continuing to serve congregations and members; the development of Free to be Faithful through online events, courses, Big Reads, and Substack; and opportunities to expand the community connected to ICS.

        Ron also reported on the Institute’s graduate programs, a more flexible education focus within the Master of Worldview Studies program, public events and institutional partnerships, and exploratory work on educational opportunities for pastors and congregational leaders. He described the work of consolidating the library collection following flooding at the Hamilton storage location, returning essential volumes to Toronto, and improving catalogue access.

        He outlined work with Kaci to strengthen fundraising and communications, the Advent Appeal, recent and anticipated major gifts, and prospective foundation grants. Members were invited to raise questions following the reports.

      7. 7 Remarks from the fundraising consultants

        Markku Kostamo of Kaci outlined a proposed $3 million capacity-building campaign based on three-year pledges. He clarified that the campaign’s primary purpose was to secure unrestricted operating support. The proposed approach included leadership and major donor conversations, church engagement, foundation applications, and a public phase once approximately 60 percent of the target had been secured. The Chair thanked Markku and the advancement team for their work.

      8. 8 Finance report

        Sandra Tang presented the financial report and the 2025–26 operating budget. She reviewed the losses in the preceding two years, donations and investment income, the use of investments to fund operations, and opportunities to review the investment portfolio and the use of restricted funds.

        The package showed a 2024–25 General Fund operating deficit of $282,074 after transfers for bursary assistance. The proposed 2025–26 budget anticipated a $55,701 deficit before the budgeted $75,902 transfer from the Okia and Reinders funds, resulting in a $20,201 surplus after that transfer.

        The following decisions were reported as approved through the electronic ballot:

        1. Appointment of Capstick McCollum & Associates as external auditor for 2025–26.
        2. Approval of the 2024–25 audited financial statements.
        3. Approval of the 2025–26 operating budget.

        The Chair reported unanimous approval in the electronic results then available. Sandra and Harley Dekker were thanked for their financial oversight. Members were offered an opportunity to ask questions.

      9. 9 Board transitions

        The Chair reported that the election of Jeremy Fisher and Laura Swan as new members of the Board of Trustees had been ratified by electronic ballot. He introduced their experience in banking, nonprofit governance, and education. Members who had not voted electronically were invited to register their votes during the meeting.

        The re-election of Elco Vandergrift and Matthew Blimke was also reported as ratified by electronic ballot.

        Dan Beerens thanked Colin Conrad for his service on the Board, particularly his advice concerning Eastern Canada and church disaffiliation. Colin expressed appreciation for the community’s support and offered to remain in contact concerning possible conversations with the Presbyterian Church in Canada.

      10. 10 Bylaw amendments

        The Chair introduced the remaining amendments to Article X of the Institute’s bylaws and reported that they had been approved by electronic ballot. Matthew Blimke was unable to attend. Ron Kuipers, Mark Standish, and Harley Dekker discussed the purpose of the amendments and the advance notice given at the previous AGM.

        The discussion described the revisions as bringing the bylaws into closer alignment with applicable legislation and the ICS Act, and modernizing procedures, including electronic communication and meetings. The Chair invited questions.

      11. 11 Closing

        Dan Beerens thanked the members for their participation and support. He offered a closing prayer for wisdom, discernment, faithfulness, and peace, and declared the meeting adjourned.

      Three nominees stand for election.       4a Heidi De Jonge

      Eastern Canada · only members in Eastern Canada vote on this seat

      Heidi De Jonge is a pastor, chaplain, and restorative practitioner based in Kingston, Ontario. She serves as a spiritual care provider at Providence Care Hospice’s AB Smith Homestead House and as an associate chaplain in Queen’s University’s Office of Faith and Spiritual Life, where her work focuses primarily on grief and loss.

      Heidi is also a trainer and coach with The Colossian Forum, helping organizations transform their conflict culture. Since 2022, she has offered restorative practices to churches experiencing conflict and trained practitioners in that work.

      Her ministry experience includes ten years as pastor of Westside Fellowship Christian Reformed Church in Kingston, earlier congregational ministry at Harderwyk CRC in Holland, Michigan, and service as Pastor for Discernment at Calvin Theological Seminary. Across these roles, she has worked in preaching, pastoral care, leadership development, vocational discernment, and community formation.

      She holds a Doctor of Ministry from Western Theological Seminary, a Master of Divinity from Calvin Theological Seminary, and a bachelor’s degree in philosophy and psychology from Dordt University. Her doctoral dissertation, “Truthing in Love: Engaging Conflict with the Disarming Love of God,” reflects her continuing interest in faith, conflict, and reconciliation.

      4b Jeremy Tinsley

      BC and Western US · only members in British Columbia and the western United States vote on this seat

      Jeremy Tinsley serves as Superintendent at Vancouver Christian School in Vancouver, BC. A graduate of Abbotsford Christian School and Dordt University, he began his career in education as a teacher at a small Christian school in rural Iowa, followed by five years at Kyiv International School in Ukraine. He and his wife later moved to Vancouver Island, where he spent 12 years as vice principal and principal at Duncan Christian School.

      Throughout his career, God has worked through the many people Jeremy has known to shape his leadership. He finds joy in supporting Christian educational communities as they equip students to know, love, and serve the Lord and others.

      Jeremy and his wife, Jen, attend Tenth Church in Vancouver and enjoy backpacking along BC’s coastal and mountain trails. They have three children: two attending university and one in grade twelve at VCS.

      4c Bob Barthel

      Member-at-Large · every member may vote on this seat

      Bob Barthel is a retired educational leader with 40 years of experience in education, including 20 years in school leadership. He holds a Bachelor of Arts from Calvin University and a Master of Worldview Studies from the Institute for Christian Studies.

      Bob served as a school principal and district principal in Christian and public school settings. He spent five years as District Principal with Red Deer Public School District, supporting educational leadership and learning across the district.

      His commitment to Christian education also took him and his family to Honduras for a year at Academia Los Pinares in partnership with World Renew. That experience broadened his perspective on the relationship between faith, learning, and community in different cultural contexts.

      Bob has served on church boards and other committees and boards, including the Ten Thousand Villages board. He brings experience in governance, organizational leadership, and community engagement, alongside a deep interest in Christian worldview and education’s contribution to the common good.

      In retirement, he looks forward to contributing his educational leadership, governance experience, international perspective, and commitment to Christian education to the mission and future of ICS.
